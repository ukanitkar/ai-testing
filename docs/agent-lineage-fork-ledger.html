A field guide to the agents ai-protect tracks

The Fork Ledger

Eighteen AI coding agents. Most of their names hide more history than they let on — forks of a shared editor, companies that split a deal two ways, a rename forced by a lawyer's letter, a product that changed its name three times in ninety days. This is what actually happened, verified against primary sources rather than assumed.

Lineage 01 — the editor family

Four forks of one editor, one split down the middle

Cursor, Windsurf, Kiro, and Antigravity are all descended from Microsoft's open-source Code OSS. Windsurf's July 2025 breakup is the one deal in this whole ledger that's routinely reported wrong — it wasn't an acquisition on either side.

Lineage 02 — AWS's own coding assistant

CodeWhisperer, twice renamed, once merged

AWS didn't fork anyone else's editor for its CLI — it kept relabeling and re-platforming its own. Kiro CLI didn't start from zero; AWS's own launch post says it "uses advanced agent functionality and technology from the Q Developer CLI."

Lineage 03 — three names in ninety days

ClawdBot → Moltbot → OpenClaw

One project, one codebase, forced to change its name twice under real pressure each time.

Lineage 04 — a name, fought over

TermAI splits into two, one keeps the name

Not every rename is a company rebranding its own product. This one is a falling-out between a solo maintainer and the team that adopted his project.

Lineage 05 — two products, deliberately not connected

xAI's Grok Build, and the "Grok CLI" that isn't

These two have no shared history at all — which is exactly the point of drawing them apart rather than together. The one this session's own grok.rs adapter almost shipped against was the wrong one.

Nine agents with no fork story

Built by the model maker for its own model, or built from scratch on purpose. No editor to fork, no rename to escape, no company to be absorbed by — the ones below are here because they were built, not because something happened to them.

ANTHROPIC · IN-HOUSE

Claude Code

The model maker's own first-party terminal client — no editor fork, nothing to fork from.

OPENAI · IN-HOUSE / REVIVED BRAND

Codex

The name is a revival: the original 2021 Codex model was discontinued in 2023, then reused in 2025 for an unrelated terminal coding agent.

GOOGLE · IN-HOUSE

Gemini CLI

Google's own terminal agent, separate from — and older than — the Windsurf-adjacent Antigravity IDE above.

MICROSOFT / GITHUB · 2021

GitHub Copilot / Copilot CLI

The original mainstream "AI pair programmer" brand — an extension across IDEs, not a fork of one.

COGNITION · IN-HOUSE

Devin CLI

Cognition's terminal surface for its autonomous-engineer product — sibling to Devin Desktop (ex-Windsurf), same company, unrelated codebase.

ZED INDUSTRIES · 2021 / RUST

Zed

Built from scratch on its own GPU-rendered UI framework by ex-Atom, ex-Tree-sitter creators — no Electron, no Code-OSS. AI (co-developed with Anthropic) was layered on in Aug 2024, four years after the editor itself.

PAUL GAUTHIER · APR 2023 · SOLO

Aider

Git-native by design — every AI edit is a real, revertable commit. Still one person's project; maintenance cadence has visibly slowed through 2026.

FORMERLY SOURCEGRAPH · NOW AMP INC.

Amp

Launched inside Sourcegraph (May 2025), spun out as its own independent company (Dec 2025). Core differentiator survived the spinout: zero markup on provider API cost.

MICROSOFT · THE FORK ROOT ITSELF

VS Code

Not tracked as its own LLM leg — ai-protect routes plain VS Code installs to the Copilot adapter instead, since that's the extension actually doing the talking.

The mergers, splits, and rebrands, in order

Every company-level event behind the names above, dated as precisely as the sources allow.

  1. Apr 30 2024

    CodeWhisperer → Amazon Q Developer. AWS folds its coding assistant into the new Q brand — a real feature superset, not just a relabel.

  2. May 2025

    Amp launches inside Sourcegraph. A research preview, not yet its own company.

  3. Jul 11 2025

    Google hires Windsurf's leadership. A reverse acqui-hire (~$2.4B): CEO Varun Mohan, co-founder Douglas Chen, and R&D staff move to Google DeepMind, plus a non-exclusive tech license. No equity, no company, no codebase.

  4. Jul 14 2025

    Cognition acquires Windsurf. Days later, Cognition buys what's left of the company outright — product, IP, brand, ~$82M ARR, 350+ enterprise customers, remaining staff.

  5. Jul 14 2025

    Kiro launches. AWS's own in-house Code-OSS fork, announced the same week as the Windsurf split — no relation to it.

  6. Nov 2025

    ClawdBot launches. Peter Steinberger's project, the start of the three-name chain.

  7. Nov 17 2025

    Kiro reaches GA; Amazon Q Developer CLI is rebranded into Kiro CLI. Inherited agent tech, not a rewrite; q commands still work.

  8. Nov 18 2025

    Antigravity launches. Google's own agent-orchestration IDE — the Windsurf-code question starts here, and stays unresolved.

  9. Dec 2025

    Amp spins out of Sourcegraph as independent Amp Inc.

  10. Jan 2026

    ClawdBot → Moltbot, forced by an Anthropic cease-and-desist over trademark similarity to "Claude."

  11. Jan 29 2026

    Moltbot → OpenClaw, 48 hours later — low recognition, crypto scammers hijacking abandoned accounts, and 1,467 malicious skills found in the registry.

  12. Feb 2 2026

    SpaceX merges with xAI — $1.25 trillion all-stock, the largest private merger ever, folding in Grok and the X platform. Tesla deliberately left out.

  13. ~Apr 30 2026

    Amazon Q Developer CLI's formal end-of-support is announced; new signups blocked May 15, full support ends April 2027.

  14. Jun 2 2026

    Windsurf → Devin Desktop, Cognition's OTA rebrand of the product it bought eleven months earlier.

  15. Jun 16 2026

    SpaceX agrees to buy Anysphere (Cursor) for $60B all-stock — the largest acquisition of a venture-backed startup ever recorded.

  16. Jun 16 2026

    Same day: Anysphere acqui-hires Continue's team. The product is discontinued; its repo goes read-only.

  17. Aug 14 2026

    The SpaceX–Anysphere deal closes — ahead of the Q3 2026 target. Cursor is now a SpaceX subsidiary, under the same roof as Grok and X.
